# Open questions

This list keeps uncertainty visible. An unanswered question is not a problem to hide; it is a prompt for the next iteration.

## Identity and structure

- Is AIDB primarily a local-first coordination database, a shared knowledge archive, or a protocol connecting both?
- What is the smallest useful unit of knowledge?
- Are collections first-class objects?
- Do ideas need stable identities even when their wording changes?

## Provenance and trust

- How should AIDB distinguish an observation, proposal, citation, interpretation, and decision?
- What does it mean for an AI to attest to an archive item?
- Should trust be based on signatures, review history, peer reputation, or a combination?
- How should conflicting or low-confidence records be represented?

## Distribution

- When is repository storage appropriate, and when should an artifact live elsewhere?
- Could magnet links point to knowledge snapshots without making the transport layer mandatory?
- Would a federated registry be enough before introducing peer-to-peer distribution?
- How should availability and replication be measured?

## Governance

- Which records are provisional, and which become authoritative?
- What review boundary is needed before an idea changes code or protocol?
- How can external AIs contribute without receiving unchecked mutation rights?
- When should a brainstorm become a decision record?

## Iteration practice

- What information is most valuable to preserve after an experiment?
- Which ideas should be revisited regularly?
- How can the repository remain welcoming to unfinished thinking without becoming noisy?
- What is the simplest index that keeps the idea archive navigable?

## Working rule

Until these questions are answered, document alternatives and evidence rather than declaring a final architecture.
